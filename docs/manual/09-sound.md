# Sound

Each player has their own sound output and their own microphone, and the two sides can also share the same speakers.

The first time DosPlex runs, Main gets the output your system already uses (the TV, for example), so you hear sound without opening the menu. Guest gets no output until Guest or Main chooses one.

## Your own output

Open **Audio** and claim a real output: TV/HDMI, speakers or a headset. Each person can claim a separate microphone too. A device already claimed by someone else is reserved for them, so release a device before the other person claims it.

An output you claim stays yours across restarts and reboots until you release it. If the device is not connected at that moment, for example a headset that is switched off, the claim stays and shows as disconnected, and the sound starts when the device appears. DosPlex never plays your sound on another device in the meantime. Microphone claims are not kept; claim the microphone again after a restart.

Guest's own Steam shows just one output, **DosPlex sound**, and one microphone, **DosPlex microphone**. The real devices are chosen in the DosPlex Audio menu; Steam's own audio settings cannot change them.

## The shared mix

For shared speakers, Main chooses a shared output and one of two mixes:

- **Full mix:** both sessions are heard through both speakers.
- **Positioned stereo mix:** you hear more of the left screen from the left speaker, and more of the right screen from the right speaker. Choose **Gentle**, Balanced or **Strong** separation. The balance follows the screen positions.
- **Stop sharing:** ends the shared mix. Each player then hears only the output they claimed, and a player who claimed none hears nothing until the mix is on again or they claim one.

A list of outputs or microphones that does not fit on one page has **Next** and **Previous** at the bottom.

Anyone who has not claimed an output of their own, usually Guest, joins the shared speakers automatically when Main starts or changes the mix. If you have claimed one, such as a headset, you keep it until you press **Join shared speakers** in your Audio menu. **Use own output** takes you out of the mix, and you stay out when Main changes it, until you join again or Main stops sharing.

The positioned stereo mix provides a speaker balance; the two people can still hear each other's audio. Headsets provide separate listening. If a game is silent, check the selected output and volume.
